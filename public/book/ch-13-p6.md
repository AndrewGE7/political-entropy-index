1. **Bipolar System:** The chapter describes the Cold War as a "bipolar system." How did this system create a thermodynamic equilibrium between the superpowers? What were the advantages and disadvantages of this equilibrium?

2. **Iron Curtain:** The Iron Curtain is presented as a thermodynamic boundary. How did this boundary function to separate the two systems? What forms of entropy flowed across the Iron Curtain, and in which directions?

3. **Nuclear Deterrence:** The chapter argues that MAD functioned as entropy suppression. How did nuclear weapons prevent direct conflict between the superpowers? What new forms of entropy did they create?

4. **Proxy Wars:** Proxy wars are described as entropy export mechanisms. How did these wars allow the superpowers to compete without direct conflict? What were the entropy costs of proxy wars for the regions where they were fought?

5. **Arms Race:** The arms race is presented as military entropy accumulation. How did the nuclear and conventional arms races consume resources and create new forms of entropy? Why did the USSR spend a higher percentage of its GDP on the arms race than the USA?

6. **Decolonization:** The chapter describes decolonization as a thermodynamic release. How did the end of empire create new entropy challenges for the emerging states? How did the superpowers compete for influence in the decolonizing world?

7. **Welfare State:** The welfare state is presented as a domestic entropy absorption mechanism. How did the welfare state reduce political entropy in Western countries? Why did the USA develop a less comprehensive welfare state than Western Europe?

8. **Space Race:** The Space Race is described as a technological entropy competition. How did the competition for space achievements reflect broader Cold War dynamics? What were the entropy costs and benefits of the Space Race?

9. **Cuban Missile Crisis:** The chapter presents the Cuban Missile Crisis as the most severe test of the Cold War entropy balance. How did the crisis come dangerously close to crossing the entropy threshold? What lessons were learned from the crisis?

10. **Vietnam War:** The Vietnam War is described as a failed entropy export. How did the war generate more entropy at home than it exported abroad? What was the Vietnam Syndrome, and how did it affect US foreign policy?

11. **Soviet Collapse:** The chapter argues that the Soviet Union collapsed under the weight of its internal entropy. What were the key entropy drivers that led to the Soviet crisis? Why did Gorbachev's reforms fail to save the system?

12. **Entropy Management:** Compare the entropy management strategies of the USA and USSR during the Cold War. Which system was more effective, and why? What lessons can we learn from these different approaches?
