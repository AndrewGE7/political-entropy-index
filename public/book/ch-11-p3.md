
3. **Geographic Proximity to Europe:**
   - **Distance:** Short sailing times from Europe
   - **Climate:** Tropical diseases initially limited penetration
   - **Rivers:** Navigation into interior (Congo, Niger, Nile)

4. **Technological Asymmetry:**
   - **Weapons:** Maxim gun, repeating rifles vs. spears
   - **Transport:** Steamships, railways vs. human porters
   - **Medicine:** Quinine (1850s) allowed European survival

**Africa as an Entropy Sink:**

European powers could **export multiple forms of entropy** to Africa:

1. **Political Entropy:**
   - **Input:** Unstable domestic politics, nationalist tensions
   - **Export:** Colonial administration, military campaigns
   - **Result:** Domestic stability maintained

2. **Economic Entropy:**
   - **Input:** Surplus capital, unemployed population
   - **Export:** Investment, settlement, infrastructure
   - **Result:** Economic volatility reduced

3. **Social Entropy:**
   - **Input:** Criminal elements, political dissidents
   - **Export:** Colonial service, military enlistment
   - **Result:** Social tension decreased

4. **Military Entropy:**
   - **Input:** Military traditions, arms industry
   - **Export:** Colonial warfare, weapons testing
   - **Result:** Military capacity demonstrated

### The Seeds of World War I: Entropy Accumulation (1900-1914)

#### The Entropy Crisis of the Edwardian Era

The period 1900-1914 witnessed a **rapid accumulation of political entropy** in the European system, as the **entropy export mechanisms** of the 19th century began to **fail** and entropy began to **boomerang back** to the core.

**Global PEI Trends (1900-1914):**

| Year | Global PEI | European PEI | Great Power PEI | Entropy Export Capacity | Systemic Stability |
|------|------------|---------------|-----------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| 1900 | 73 | 62 | 64 | High | Stable |
| 1905 | 74 | 63 | 65 | Medium | Warning |
| 1910 | 75 | 65 | 67 | Low | Fragile |
| 1914 | 76 | 66 | 68 | None | Crisis |

**Key Entropy Accumulation Factors:**

1. **Colonial Saturation:** By 1900, **90% of Africa** and **56% of Asia** were under European control—**no new entropy sinks** available
2. **Rising Nationalism:** Nationalist movements in colonies and Europe **resisted entropy export**
3. **Economic Interdependence:** Global trade networks **transmitted entropy** between countries
4. **Military Technology:** Industrial warfare made **conflict more destructive**
5. **Social Unrest:** Labor movements, women's suffrage, and socialist parties **increased domestic entropy**

#### The Failure of Entropy Export Mechanisms

**Mechanism 1: Colonialism**

By 1914, colonialism was **no longer an effective entropy export mechanism**:

| Problem | Explanation | Entropy Effect |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| Colonial Saturation | Most of the world was already colonized | No new entropy sinks |
| Nationalist Resistance | Colonial subjects increasingly resisted | Entropy boomerang |
| Economic Costs | Colonial administration was expensive | Domestic PEI ↑ |
| Moral Opposition | Anti-colonial movements in Europe | Political tension ↑ |
| Resource Dependence | European economies depended on colonial resources | Vulnerability ↑ |

**Example:** Britain's **Indian Empire** (the "Jewel in the Crown"):
- **1858:** Direct rule established after Indian Rebellion
- **1900:** India provided **£30 million/year** in tax revenue and **£50 million/year** in trade surplus
- **1905:** Partition of Bengal → **Swadeshi movement** (boycott of British goods)
- **1914:** India contributed **1.5 million soldiers** to WWI
- **Result:** India's **PEI increased from 85 to 90**, and British **dependency on India increased**, making Britain **more vulnerable to Indian entropy**

**Mechanism 2: Migration**

Migration, another entropy export mechanism, also **became less effective**:

| Migration Flow | 1876-1900 | 1900-1914 | Change | Reason |
|----------------|-----------|-----------|--------|--------|
| Europe → USA | 15 million | 13 million | -13% | US immigration restrictions |
| Europe → Latin America | 5 million | 3 million | -40% | Latin American nationalism |
| Europe → Africa/Asia | 2 million | 1 million | -50% | Colonial resistance |
| Rural → Urban (Europe) | 20 million | 25 million | +25% | Industrial growth |
| **Total** | **42 million** | **42 million** | **0%** | **Stagnation** |

**Migration Entropy Effect:**
- **1876-1900:** Migration **reduced European PEI** by exporting surplus population
- **1900-1914:** Migration **stagnated**, and **urbanization increased domestic entropy**

**Mechanism 3: Trade**

Global trade, which had **dissipated economic entropy**, began to **transmit it**:

| Trade Flow | 1876 | 1900 | 1914 | Entropy Effect |
|------------|------|------|------|----------------|
| Europe → World | 60% | 55% | 50% | Declining dominance |
| World → Europe | 40% | 45% | 50% | Increasing dependence |
| Primary Products | 60% | 50% | 40% | Resource scarcity |
| Manufactured Goods | 40% | 50% | 60% | Industrial competition |

```
Global Trade ↑
    ↓

    ↓
```

- **Trigger:** Speculation in railroad stocks
- **Transmission:** European investors pulled capital from USA
- **Effect:** Financial crisis in USA **spread to Europe**
- **Result:** Global **PEI increase of 2-3 points**

#### The Entropy Boomerang: How Exported Entropy Returned

By 1914, the **entropy boomerang effect** was in full swing:

**Boomerang 1: Colonial Resistance**

Colonial subjects, inspired by **European nationalism**, began to **resist entropy export**:

| Resistance Form | Example | European Impact |
|----------------|---------|-----------------|
| Nationalist Movements | Indian National Congress (1885) | Moral pressure, political tension |
| Armed Revolts | Maji Maji Rebellion (1905-1907) | Military casualties, financial cost |
| Boycotts | Swadeshi Movement (1905) | Economic losses, political embarrassment |
| Intellectual Critique | Pan-African Congress (1900) | Ideological challenge |

**Result:** Colonial resistance **increased European PEI** by:
- **Military costs** (1-2 PEI points)
- **Political tension** (1-2 PEI points)
- **Economic losses** (1 PEI point)

**Boomerang 2: Economic Dependence**

European powers became **dependent on colonial resources**, making them **vulnerable to entropy import**:

| Resource | Colonial Source | European Dependency | Vulnerability |
|----------|-----------------|----------------------|--------------|

| Palm Oil | West Africa | Soap, lubricants | Market control |

**Example:** Britain's **cotton dependency**:
- **1876:** 75% of British cotton came from **India**
- **1900:** 60% came from **India**, 25% from **Egypt**
- **1914:** 50% came from **India**, 30% from **Egypt**, 20% from **USA**
- **Vulnerability:** Any disruption in **India or Egypt** would **cripple British textile industry**
- **PEI Effect:** **Increased British vulnerability to Indian/Egyptian entropy**
