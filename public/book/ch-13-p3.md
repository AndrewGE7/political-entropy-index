**Why the USSR Spent More:**

The USSR **spent a higher percentage of its GDP** on the arms race because:

1. **Economic Inefficiency:** The **command economy** was **less efficient** at producing **consumer goods**
2. **Technological Gap:** The USSR was **behind** the USA in **many technologies**, requiring **greater investment** to **catch up**
3. **Strategic Necessity:** The USSR **perceived itself as vulnerable** and **needed to match** US capabilities
4. **Political Pressure:** The **military-industrial complex** in the USSR had **significant political power**

**The MIRV Revolution (1960s-1970s):**

The development of **Multiple Independently Targetable Reentry Vehicles (MIRVs)** in the **late 1960s and early 1970s** **dramatically increased** the **destructive capacity** of nuclear arsenals:

- **Pre-MIRV:** 1 missile = 1 warhead = 1 target
- **Post-MIRV:** 1 missile = 10-14 warheads = 10-14 targets

**MIRV Impact:**
- **Increased Destructive Capacity:** A single **Minuteman III** missile could carry **3 warheads**; a **Trident II** SLBM could carry **8-14**
- **Enhanced Second-Strike Capability:** MIRVs made it **harder to destroy** enough missiles to **prevent retaliation**
- **Escalated Arms Race:** Both sides **rushed to deploy** MIRVs, **accelerating the arms race**
- **Arms Control Challenges:** MIRVs made **verification** of arms control agreements **more difficult**

**The Military-Industrial Complex:**

The **military-industrial complex**—the **alliance between military leaders and defense industries**—was a **major driver** of the arms race in both superpowers:

5. **Strategic Calculus:** Both sides **feared falling behind** in the arms race

**Conclusion:** The **arms race** was a **major mechanism** of **military entropy accumulation** during the Cold War. It **consumed vast resources**, **accelerated technological development**, and **created a constant risk of escalation**. While **arms control agreements** had **some success**, they **could not stop** the **fundamental competition** between the superpowers.

### Decolonization: Entropy Redistribution and New States

#### The End of Empire: Thermodynamic Release

The **decolonization** of Africa and Asia (1945-1989) was one of the **most significant entropy redistributions** of the Cold War. As **European empires collapsed**, **new states** emerged, **reshaping the global system** and **creating new opportunities and challenges** for the superpowers.

**Decolonization as Thermodynamic Process:**

Decolonization can be understood as a **thermodynamic release**—the **sudden removal** of **imperial control** (order) allowed **pent-up entropy** (nationalism, economic exploitation, political repression) to **explode** into **new state formation**.

**First Law Application:**
```
ΔU_colony = -Q_imperial_control + W_nationalist_movement
```

Where:
- **ΔU_colony** = Change in the colony's internal energy (capacity for self-governance)
- **Q_imperial_control** = Energy input from imperial power (administration, military, economic extraction)
- **W_nationalist_movement** = Work done by nationalist movement (resistance, organization, diplomacy)

**When Q_imperial_control < W_nationalist_movement, independence becomes inevitable.**

#### The Entropy of New States

The **new states** that emerged from decolonization **faced significant entropy challenges**:

1. **Political Entropy:**
   - **Lack of Experience:** Most new states had **no experience** with **self-governance**
   - **Artificial Borders:** Colonial borders **did not reflect** ethnic or cultural realities
   - **Ethnic Tensions:** Multi-ethnic states **struggled with** internal divisions
   - **PEI Impact:** **+10-20 points** (high political instability)

2. **Economic Entropy:**
   - **Resource Dependence:** Many new states **depended on** a **single commodity** (oil, minerals, cash crops)
   - **Lack of Infrastructure:** Colonial powers had **underinvested** in **education, industry, transportation**
   - **Brain Drain:** Many **educated elites** had **emigrated** or been **co-opted by colonial powers**
   - **PEI Impact:** **+5-15 points** (economic vulnerability)

3. **Social Entropy:**
   - **Education Gap:** Low **literacy rates**, limited **technical skills**
   - **Health Challenges:** High **disease rates**, low **life expectancy**
   - **Urbanization:** Rapid **migration to cities** created **slums and unemployment**
   - **PEI Impact:** **+5-10 points** (social instability)

4. **Security Entropy:**
   - **Weak Militaries:** New states had **limited defense capabilities**
   - **Internal Threats:** **Coups, rebellions, civil wars** were common
   - **External Threats:** **Neighboring states, former colonial powers, superpowers** posed risks
   - **PEI Impact:** **+5-15 points** (security vulnerability)

#### Cold War Competition in the Decolonizing World

The **decolonizing world** became a **major battleground** in the Cold War, as both superpowers **sought to bring** new states into their **spheres of influence**.

**Superpower Strategies:**

1. **USA Strategy:**
   - **Economic Aid:** **Marshall Plan** (Europe), **Foreign Aid** (developing world)
   - **Military Alliances:** **NATO**, **CENTO**, **SEATO**
   - **Political Support:** Support for **pro-Western leaders**
   - **Cultural Influence:** **Education, media, consumer culture**

2. **USSR Strategy:**
   - **Economic Aid:** **COMECON**, **bilateral aid**
   - **Military Alliances:** **Warsaw Pact**, **bilateral treaties**
   - **Political Support:** Support for **communist parties** and **revolutionary movements**
   - **Cultural Influence:** **Propaganda, education, technical assistance**

**The Non-Aligned Movement:**

Many new states **chose not to align** with either superpower, forming the **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)** in **1961**. The NAM **sought to**:

1. **Avoid Superpower Domination:** Maintain **independence** from both USA and USSR
2. **Promote Economic Development:** Focus on **economic cooperation** among developing nations
3. **Advocate for Peace:** Push for **nuclear disarmament** and **conflict resolution**
4. **Support Decolonization:** Assist **remaining colonies** in achieving independence
